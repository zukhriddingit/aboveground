# Park Street field sources

## Plot and survey

- The final plot is a 240 m square centered at 42.3566005° N, 71.0619941° W, turned −63.1° from the east–north survey grid. It contains the five named buildings and all five modeled station structures. The source is the supplied extract, dated November 22, 2026 UTC. [OpenStreetMap contributors](https://www.openstreetmap.org/#map=19/42.3566005/-71.0619941)
- The initial −35° square clipped two sides of the Granary. In the final square, 6,990 of its approximately 7,013 m² are on the plate. One rear corner continues past the edge. The gate, street frontage, Franklin monument and five named buildings are whole. A geometrical intersection check of the surrounding tagged landmark buildings found zero partial intersections. The small gap between the Granary and the Athenæum constrains this frame. [Granary boundary](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/29861355) · [Boston Parks survey plan](https://www.boston.gov/sites/default/files/embed/m/most_recent_granary_map.pdf)
- The prepared scene contains 68 mapped buildings and building parts, 90 mapped trees, 8 crossing markings, the fountain pool and 17 named shops and places. Five building records have neither a height nor a level count. Twenty-six ordinary building records meet the edge. [OpenStreetMap survey](https://www.openstreetmap.org/#map=19/42.3566005/-71.0619941)
- Each of the two granite kiosks uses its own mapped footprint. The Common-side thresholds locate their open ends. [West kiosk](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/24677664) · [East kiosk](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/24677688) · [Exit threshold](https://www.openstreetmap.org/node/3798697873) · [Entrance threshold](https://www.openstreetmap.org/node/3798697872)
- The 5 m glass lift, projecting metal canopy and pair of glass-block stairs have separate survey outlines. The accessible door is at the projecting canopy. [Glass lift](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/846047438) · [Canopy](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/846047441) · [Lift threshold](https://www.openstreetmap.org/node/7893246729) · [North stairs](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/818470230) · [South stairs](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/818470232)
- The kit reads the 30.2 m MassGIS Tremont Street width as a carriageway, covering the separately mapped sidewalks. The lengths of the Bromfield and Winter Street crossings give approximately 14.5 m and 17.0 m across the pavement respectively. The local preparation script uses those two widths and keeps the original extract intact. [Tremont Street](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/63104590) · [Bromfield crossing](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/383026774) · [Winter Street crossing](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/376449306)
- The Common master plan shows the **existing** concrete vending plaza, headhouses, ornamental fence, bollards and the Freedom Trail. This informed the paved area between the kiosks and Park Street. The current mapped grass boundaries give the edge of its planted beds. [Boston Common Master Plan, pp. 94–95](https://www.boston.gov/sites/default/files/file/2023/05/bmcp-full-report.pdf)
- The red strip follows six through-going footway members of the Freedom Trail relation. At the edge of the plate its two separate lengths continue towards the State House. The relation also lists whole site outlines, which are not stretches of red pavement. [Freedom Trail route geometry](https://www.openstreetmap.org/relation/976514) · [National Park Service photograph of the brick line](https://www.nps.gov/places/freedom-trail-start.htm)

## Station photographs and drawings

- Wheelwright and Haven designed the 1897 station. The two granite kiosks with copper roofs and the later transit structures are documented by the architectural survey. [Society of Architectural Historians](https://sah-archipedia.org/buildings/MA-01-BD1)
- The 1970s federal nomination identifies the classical entrance and exit kiosks on the Common. The drawings here use the later street photographs for their visible fittings. [National Park Service, Tremont Street Subway nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/cc3a418b-95a0-41f8-bf6b-7ec357e2fd43)
- The plan, section and exterior photograph of the Leers Weinzapfel surface structures show steel posts, glass block, granite bases and a standing-seam roof. [Progressive Architecture, April 1991, “Selected Detail”](https://www.usmodernist.org/PA/PA-1991-04.pdf)
- The photograph by Eric Kilby shows three high windows on the kiosk end, the cornice, and the relationship of the dark hip roof to the church clock tower. CC BY-SA 2.0, June 2008. [Park Street before dawn](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Park_Street_headhouse_and_Park_Street_Church,_June_2008.jpg)
- Mmurphy6’s daylight side elevation shows three pairs of clerestory windows with X muntins, recessed granite panels, dentils, a standing-seam hip roof, and a wheeled cart with hanging wares under a red-and-green umbrella. CC BY-SA 4.0, October 2020. [The headhouse to Park St. station](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_headhouse_to_Park_St._station.jpg)
- Ethan Long’s snow photograph shows the roof seams, end windows and snowy plaza. CC BY-SA 2.0, January 2009. [Park Street Station during a snowstorm](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Park_Street_Station_on_Tremont_during_snowstorm_(53812930763).jpg)
- Ethan Long’s view along the opposite sidewalk shows glass blocks in dark steel bays over granite plinths. CC BY-SA 2.0, September 2008. [MBTA station exits on Tremont Street](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:MBTA_Station_Exits_on_Tremont_Street_(53829362764).jpg)
- Bynyalcin’s Tremont crossing photograph shows the enameled T roundel, brick and stone shopfronts, glazed shop doors, streetlights and the bowed law-school frontage. CC BY 3.0, September 2014. [Crosswalk at Park Street station](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Crosswalk_on_Tremont_Street_at_Park_Street_station,_September_2014.jpg)

## Buildings, gate and fountain

- Peter Banner’s 1809 church has a 217-foot steeple. The drawing uses the mapped clock tower, square belfry and successive octagonal upper stages, tapering to the vane. The daylight and predawn views guided the proportions of the brick nave and the white trim. [Freedom Trail Foundation](https://www.thefreedomtrail.org/index.php/trail-sites/park-street-church) · [HABS exterior survey, Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/item/ma0913/) · [National Park Service exterior view](https://npgallery.nps.gov/AssetDetail/7191c273-6b57-4e9e-b457-303c52599525) · [Tower study photograph](https://www.cynic.org.uk/photos/USA2006/Boston/FreedomTrail/index2.html)
- Boston Parks documents the 1840 granite wall, gateway and cast-iron fence and publishes the burial plan. The entrance sits where the mapped internal walk meets Tremont. The Franklin parents’ monument is a 25-foot obelisk. Its position comes from the mapped memorial. [Fence restoration, with photographs](https://www.boston.gov/sites/default/files/embed/h/historic-burying-ground-march.pdf) · [City burial plan](https://www.boston.gov/sites/default/files/embed/m/most_recent_granary_map.pdf) · [City cemetery history](https://www.boston.gov/cemeteries/granary-burying-ground) · [Franklin marker](https://www.openstreetmap.org/node/13710038664)
- A street photograph documents the gateway’s battered stone posts, winged disk and flanking ironwork. An elevated archive view places it in the fence line. [American Society of Landscape Architects](https://www.asla.org/guide/Site.aspx?id=40132) · [Tufts Digital Library](https://dl.tufts.edu/concern/images/4j03d863n)
- St Paul’s has a Quincy granite body, six Ionic sandstone columns and a Greek-temple portico. The modern pediment holds Donald Lipski’s cross-section of a nautilus on a blue field. [Cathedral building history](https://stpaulboston.org/200-years-of-st-pauls/tag/architecture) · [Diocesan design rendering](https://www.diomass.org/diocesan-news/something-old-something-new-cathedral-church-st-paul-unveils-bold-proposal) · [Cathedral exterior photograph](https://stpaulboston.org/what-we-do)
- Brewer Fountain is a French bronze cast, given to Boston in 1868. Four water figures sit around its base. The historic view, restoration photographs and mapped basin supply the bowls, socle, pedestal and coping. [Friends of the Public Garden restoration](https://friendsofthepublicgarden.org/what-we-do/capital-projects/brewer-fountain/) · [Friends’ sculpture guide](https://friendsofthepublicgarden.org/our-parks/the-common/) · [Historic New England photograph](https://www.historicnewengland.org/explore/collections-access/gusn/198340/) · [Mapped fountain](https://www.openstreetmap.org/node/358276809)
- Sargent Hall's project architect describes a limestone front with a five-story bow, two-story entry arcade and a continuous stainless-steel overhang above the upper loggia. The model follows the curved survey footprint to the Hamilton Place and Bromfield sides. [David M. Owens, project photographs and description](https://www.dmowens.com/)
- The Orpheum's entrance is at the end of Hamilton Place. The façade elevation and historic and recent street photographs informed the classical relief, narrow approach and projecting marquee. [1922 elevation, Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/item/2005676637/) · [Methuen Memorial Music Hall, illustrated history](https://mmmh.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/THE-BOSTON-MUSIC-HALL_-FROM-THEN-1.pdf) · [WBUR photographs](https://www.wbur.org/news/2024/09/05/orpheum-theatre-alley-lawsuit-holocaust-museum-boston)
- The light-faced R. H. Stearns department store has large window bays and a projecting cornice. The surviving marquee carries the Stearns name. The current footprint provides ten levels and a height of 41 m. [Historical Forrest Orr advertising postcard](https://www.cardcow.com/719524/boston-massachusetts-rh-stearns-company/) · [Adam Gaffin’s photographs of the canopy](https://www.universalhub.com/2014/downtowns-other-lost-department-store) · [OSM building](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/29605125)

## Seasons and occasions

- Spring uses fresh leaves, soft rain, umbrellas and folded café parasols. Boston Logan's 1991–2020 April mean daily maximum is 56.4°F. [NOAA monthly normals, USW00014739](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=normals-monthly-1991-2020&endDate=9996-12-31&format=pdf&startDate=0001-01-01&stations=USW00014739)
- Summer uses full green crowns and open plaza furniture. The July mean daily maximum at Logan is 82.1°F. [NOAA monthly normals](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=normals-monthly-1991-2020&endDate=9996-12-31&format=pdf&startDate=0001-01-01&stations=USW00014739)
- Fall uses ochre and rust foliage. The October mean daily maximum at Logan is 62.1°F. [NOAA monthly normals](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=normals-monthly-1991-2020&endDate=9996-12-31&format=pdf&startDate=0001-01-01&stations=USW00014739)
- Winter strips the deciduous branches, closes the fountain and adds snow to roofs, paving and grass. January averages 14.3 inches of snow at Logan. [NOAA monthly normals](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/services/data/v1?dataset=normals-monthly-1991-2020&endDate=9996-12-31&format=pdf&startDate=0001-01-01&stations=USW00014739) · [Snow at the station](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Park_Street_Station_on_Tremont_during_snowstorm_(53812930763).jpg)
- The Friends of the Public Garden program portable-piano lunchtime jazz at Brewer Plaza. A participating performer's photograph shows a black piano under a yellow canopy. The miniature has an anonymous pianist and listeners beside the fountain. [Friends of the Public Garden](https://friendsofthepublicgarden.org/what-we-do/capital-projects/brewer-fountain/) · [Performer’s account and photographs](https://www.annalotte.com/blog/where-the-people-are)
- The Foundation runs an after-dark lantern tour from Boston Common with a cloaked guide. The scene places the guide and listeners on the red trail outside the burying ground. [Freedom Trail Foundation tour](https://www.thefreedomtrail.org/tours/lantern) · [Foundation tour brochure](https://www.thefreedomtrail.org/sites/default/files/content/PDFs/Freedom%20Trail%20Brochure%202025.pdf)

## Interpretation

Mapped coordinates determine the outlines, roads, walks, relative building positions, tree positions and basin. The five unmapped heights, unsurveyed architectural profiles, window bays, shop fascias, paint colours, individual headstones and sculptural figures are illustrative. No headstone inscription is invented. The cart is taken from a dated photograph. Foliage, weather, road users, number of visitors, café seating and the exact positions of the piano and tour party describe a representative scene. Source photos are drawing references only. Map data is © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL.
